# Super Over

## Definition

A Super Over is a tie-breaker method used in the Indian Premier League (IPL) when a match ends in a tie after both teams have completed their respective innings. Each team plays a shortened, one-over innings to determine the winner.

## Purpose

This rule was introduced to eliminate tied matches in limited-overs cricket, ensuring an exciting and definitive result for spectators and the tournament standings without relying on coin tosses or boundary counts.

## Key Rules

* Each team nominates three batsmen and one bowler for the Super Over.
* The team that batted second in the main match bats first in the Super Over.
* The fielding team is allowed a maximum of six legal deliveries or until two wickets fall, whichever occurs first.
* If the scores remain tied after both teams complete their Super Over, subsequent Super Overs are played until a winner is determined.
* Extras such as wides and no-balls apply as in standard overs.

## Example

In the 2019 IPL final between Mumbai Indians and Chennai Super Kings, Chennai tied Mumbai's total of 149. In the subsequent Super Over, Chennai was restricted to 5/1 chasing Mumbai's target of 10 runs, making Mumbai the champions.

## Recent Changes

Originally, if subsequent Super Overs were also tied, the match would be decided by counting the total number of boundaries hit during the main match and the Super Over. Following criticism after the 2019 ICC Men's Cricket World Cup final, the IPL and ICC updated the rule to allow unlimited Super Overs until a clear winner emerges.
